# Baseline output rules

Applies to `baseline_algo/` and its generated outputs.

# Sequential 3+2 experiment (user decision, 2026-09-26)

- `run_sequential.py` runs ten pose1 particles, each selecting three fixed 1% heads with the existing top5 sampling. For each viable triple, choose the geometrically legal member with greatest singleton coverage gain on pose2, then sample two new pose2 heads. Each completed layout has five distinct centers and exactly one shared head.
- Score and check common withdrawal/basic connectivity for each task's active triple. Generate task-local candidate pools; do not restore the old requirement that every head contact both tasks or avoid the other task's work surface. The shared head itself must be legal in both and retain the same contact surface and head solid under the object transform.
- Inactive material is not checked or certified as a foot. Success means both active triples cover the original loads; complete fixture placement, inactive-part collisions, feet and whole-body withdrawal remain Step5 work.
- Preserve the terminal expansion rule below, updating a shared head consistently in both tasks. A pose1 triple at or below 98% can be rejected before transfer, since the retained terminal rule cannot rescue it. With expansion disabled, pose1 must be fully covered before transfer.
- Store results under each existing pair stage in `sequential_3plus2/from_<first_pose>/terminal_expansion/` (or `fixed_only/` with `--no-expansion`). Pair order matters. Preserve the earlier joint-five-head outputs.

# Step3 contact area rule (user decision, 2026-09-26)

- During selection in the active two-pose baseline, every head has a fixed area of 1% of the total object surface, within the existing relative area fitting tolerance of 1e-4.
- Fit candidate radii once during candidate generation. Reject candidates that cannot reach the target or whose geometry is invalid at that area; do not shrink them to rescue eligibility.
- Each selection round only adds a head using the existing top5 sampling. Do not resize during selection, use coverage/area as an objective, or minimize area after sample completion. Contact-area optimization is deferred to the future structural-geometry stage.
- After selection stops, preserve an already complete chain. Otherwise, only if EACH pose covers strictly more than 98% of its original samples, try terminal expansion at the same centers and head count. Use target areas 1.01%, 1.02%, 1.05%, 1.10% of the object surface; no shrinking, new heads or added loads. Recheck mechanics, common insertion and basic connectivity. Stop at full sample completion; below/equal to the threshold or exhausted growth remains a failure.
- Keep the fixed-load acceptance, shared no-uplift, insertion and basic connectivity rules below. Candidate diagnostics, value networks and shared-entity construction are outside this change.
- Current outputs use `fixed_area_1pct/terminal_expansion/` inside each existing stage, with explicit head-budget runs in its `heads_<n>/` subdirectory. The fixed-only and earlier radius-optimized outputs remain historical comparisons.

# Load acceptance rule (user decision, 2026-09-26)

- Use exactly the fixed 32,768 Step1 sampled loads per pose for scoring and completion. A design passes once every sample in every task passes the existing reaction constraints.
- Do not run continuous-load-domain certification, search for extra load counterexamples, or append extra loads. Do not make a separate zero-process-force gravity test an additional completion gate; gravity is already included in each sampled demand.
- Preserve the per-sample shared no-uplift condition, contact geometry, common head insertion directions and basic connectivity checks.
- This rule supersedes older Markdown requirements for continuous-domain proof. Historical single-pose/continuous-proof modules are reference code, not the current acceptance workflow; do not invoke their old continuous validation when extending the active baseline.
