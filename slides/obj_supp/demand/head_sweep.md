Their outward offset head cells have the same geometry as the baseline withdrawal analysis. All three patches lie outside the work faces (green). Head colours identify the three heads; all text is black and English.

- **(a) Separate heads:** sparse arrows show insertion toward each head, reversing the certified withdrawal directions. Normal-like, oblique, horizontal and sideways directions are represented together. The lower direction chart also plots insertion vectors, the negatives of the withdrawal vectors; its elevations therefore run from -90 to 0 degrees. No screen-side or projected-length filter is applied. Greedy farthest-angle sampling starts with the certified ray nearest the contact-center normal, then adds rays until every catalogue ray is within 27 degrees of a displayed ray. This produces 10, 8, 8 orange, blue and purple arrows respectively, with maximum nearest-arrow angles of 24.83, 23.98, 25.14 degrees respectively. Every displayed full ray is independently replayed clear. Arrows now point inward and end at distinct actual head-surface vertices around the whole block; their lengths are adjusted for readability while preserving their 3-D directions. The orange, blue and purple heads have 301, 240 and 239 certified catalogue rays respectively. The lower plot displays every catalogue ray as a point; the angular cover does not certify unsampled directions between them.
- **(b) Rigidly connected heads:** a broad teal belt joins all three heads. Each attachment extends the entire head cross-section into the belt instead of using a thin central pin. The first 8 mm retain the contact colour, and the green rib starts at 7 mm, giving a 1 mm overlapping connection. The far end overlaps the 10 mm wide, 5 mm thick belt. A Boolean union of the three heads and all connectors is verified to be one watertight solid. Installed connector meshes have no volumetric intersection with the workpiece. This panel shows only the connected assembly, with no escape arrows or black cross marks. Every head must still translate with the same vector; the obstruction follows from the direction proof below. This is a connection illustration, not a strength or manufacturability result.


Directions for this illustration are recomputed from full head-versus-workpiece/floor sweep tests over the above-floor catalogue. The Step2 area-averaged work-side hemisphere prefilter is omitted: it incorrectly removes physically clear upward escape directions for this geometric illustration. The original Step2 output is not modified. The orange head's pure upward ray is explicitly checked clear. This figure checks collision with the actual workpiece (including its work faces), not clearance from a separate external tool-workspace volume.

Left-panel arrows use 3-D stems and camera-facing triangular tips in the same depth buffer as the bunny. The triangular tips prevent end-on cones from appearing as circular dots, so an arrow behind the work surface is occluded instead of being painted on top of it. Every displayed direction is independently replayed as a full-head sweep. Right-panel arrows are omitted.

Horizontal withdrawal remains included. Here “horizontal” means zero vertical component in world coordinates, parallel to the ground. A camera looking down onto the horizontal plane can project such a vector as a slanted arrow. Normal-like and sideways arrows are displayed simultaneously, using the same selection rule for all directions.

The absence of a common straight direction does **not** follow just from the individual arrow fans or the finite point sets. Let N contain every outward face normal of all three actual contact patches. Any infinitesimal non-penetrating withdrawal d must satisfy N d >= 0. Six linear programs maximize the positive and negative coordinate components of d over this cone intersected with [-1,1]^3; every optimum is zero. In addition, the script finds nonnegative combinations of N's rows that reconstruct each of the six signed coordinate axes, with reconstruction error below 1e-10. Each certificate has exactly three active normals. Their stored IEEE floating-point components are converted with `Fraction.from_float`, the 3×3 system is solved by exact rational elimination, and all three weights are asserted strictly positive. The rational reconstruction of the signed coordinate axis is asserted exactly equal, without a numerical tolerance. Exact weights are saved in the JSON. Thus the certificate is exact for the stored mesh-normal values (not an assertion of exact physical surface geometry). These positive-span certificates independently show that N d >= 0 forces d = 0. No floor or work-face direction restriction is required for this obstruction. Thus no nonzero **straight translation at fixed orientation** can start from the installed pose, and reversing a straight insertion cannot solve it either. This example makes no claim about paths containing rotations, deformation, or head disassembly.

The proof concerns the given finite surface-matched patches and their actual face normals. It is not a claim that ideal point contacts with opposite normals necessarily lock all translations; tangent directions can remain for point contacts.
